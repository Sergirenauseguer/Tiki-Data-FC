# Tiki-Data — Frame-level ball possession, FC Barcelona – Athletic Club

More than a Hack 2027, Phase 1 · Technical document · Team **Tiki-Data**

Submission file: `submission/Tiki-Data.csv` (199,319 rows, `H` / `A` / `D`). Regenerated byte for byte by
`python run.py` (see §6).

---

## 1. Approach

The ranking metric, spellF1, scores **possessions as objects**: a predicted possession only counts if it overlaps a
true one of the same team by temporal IoU ≥ 0.3 … 0.7. Two consequences shaped the design:

- **Segmentation matters more than per-frame accuracy.** A flicker invents a possession and can split a true one.
- **Dead-ball frames are not possessions.** `D` separates possessions but is never matched itself, so on a
  doubtful stretch it is better to say nothing than to predict a possession that will not match.

The pipeline has four layers.

**Layer 1 — Physics of the ball and dead-ball rules.** From the tracking alone: ball outside the pitch or lost at a
line while moving outwards, kick-offs (half starts and after goals, located with the Smart Tagging), the goalkeeper
holding the ball, players stopping as a whole (90th percentile of player speed). The output is a per-frame
dead/alive state and a first team call (nearest player and confirmed controls).

**Layer 2 — A learned frame model, trained only on other matches.** A LightGBM classifier gives, for every frame,
P(dead) and P(home has the ball) from ~110 team-level signals: ball position, speed and visibility, gaps in the ball
track, nearest players of each team and their distances, team shape (centroid, width, length), player speeds,
recent controls and the layer-1 states. It is trained on external matches **degraded to look like the provided
tracking** (ball missing in the same proportion and in blocks, positional noise and drift, smoothing), so that it
learns under the target's conditions. A second model gives P(possession change), and a third, using players only,
gives P(dead) when the ball is not visible. Home/away are swapped as data augmentation.

**Sequence model for the team in possession.** P(home has the ball) comes from the mean of two sequence models
(1-D convolution followed by a bidirectional GRU) that read the same ~110 signals at 5 fps over windows of 1 and
2 minutes, so each frame is decided with the context of the play around it. They are trained on the 7 IDSSE matches
(degraded copies) and give a better team call than the per-frame classifier, especially in possessions of 2-8 s.
The two trained models are in `models/seq/`; inference runs on CPU and gives the same file as on GPU.

**Layer 3 — Choosing whole possessions.** Instead of a single Viterbi path, 28 decodings of the frame model with
different switch and stoppage costs produce candidate possessions. A gradient-boosted scorer, trained out of
sample on the external matches, predicts for each candidate how well it will match a true possession (its expected
match value over the IoU thresholds), from its duration, the model's confidence inside and at its edges, ball
visibility, controls of each team, kicks at its start and how many decodings agree on it. An exact weighted interval
selection keeps the non-overlapping set that maximises Σ(2p − λ), since adding a possession with match probability
p raises F1 when 2p exceeds the current F1. Everything not selected is `D`.

**Layer 4 — Rules of the game on the selected possessions** (§4). General football rules, applied after the
selection, with no constant tied to a frame of the match.

## 2. Assets used and dropped

| asset | use |
|---|---|
| Tactical ATD (players and ball) | everything: layers 1-4 |
| Smart Tagging | kick-off and goal intervals only (layer 1) |
| Tactical homography | not needed (the ATD is already in pitch coordinates) |
| Broadcast homography | a per-frame "replay / close-up" flag (the homography is invalid when the director cuts away from the live pitch), aligned to the tactical clock with a constant per-half lag found from the two ball tracks (layer 4) |
| Tactical and broadcast video | inspected to understand the data; no model reads them |

Tried and dropped (measured on the external matches): sequence-free decoders with variable costs only; ensembles
of LightGBM seeds; several extra scorer features (sustained control, ball in flight, stoppage reasons); candidates
from more sources; self-training on the target with its own confident predictions; correcting the team of the
tracklets with an 11-versus-11 consistency check; a ball detector run on the tactical video (pre-trained COCO
model): its detections were less precise than interpolating across the gap; Smart Tagging phases as a team prior.

## 3. Training data

No data about the provided game is used for training, tuning or model selection.

| source | matches | labels | who labelled | licence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| IDSSE / Sportec Open DFL dataset (Bassek et al., 2025) | 7 Bundesliga (also the only training data of the sequence models) | per-frame ball possession and ball status from the provider | the data provider (DFL / Sportec) | CC BY 4.0 |
| Metrica Sports sample data | 3 | possession and dead ball reconstructed from the provider's event data | the data provider (Metrica Sports) | Metrica sample-data terms |

Two degraded copies of each external match are used. The possession label is aligned to *controlled* possession as
defined by the challenge: a spell of the opponent shorter than 2 s that does not string passes is not a change of
possession. All choices (costs, thresholds of the selector, which rules enter) were made by cross-validation
leaving one external match out, with a nested estimate for the choice itself.

## 4. Possession rules implemented

| situation | rule |
|---|---|
| Loose ball, deflection, blocked pass, aerial duel | possession stays with the last team in control until the other team controls the ball (switch costs in the decoder; a change needs evidence over time, not a single touch) |
| A stoppage needs a reason | a short `D` between two possessions with no physical reason (ball out, restart position, play stopped) is not a stoppage: between possessions of the same team it is filled; between different teams it is split |
| Goalkeeper catches the ball | the ball lost inside the area near the goalkeeper of the team that plays next, without leaving the pitch, is that team's possession, not a stoppage |
| Set pieces | ball still at a corner, the penalty spot, the goal area, the centre spot or the touchline, coming from a stoppage: still `D` until the kick; after the kick the possession belongs to the team that takes it |
| Restart wait | after a stoppage, the ball still and the opponents far away (> 12 m) means play has not restarted yet |
| False restart | after a restart, if the ball stops again without a second touch, the stoppage continues |
| Short unexplained stoppage | a very short `D` without a reason is absorbed by the neighbouring possession |
| Live picture | at the edges of a stoppage, with no replay on the broadcast, the ball moving and players running, play is still on |
| Outside the halves | before each kick-off, at half-time and after the final whistle: `D` |

## 5. Limitations

- **Short possessions (2-8 s) are the main loss.** In the external matches about 90 % of our possessions match a
  true one, but a quarter of the true ones are missed, almost all short: in them the team in possession is the
  team nearest to the ball only about half of the time, so the tracking does not show who controls it.
- **Stoppage edges.** Most remaining errors are `D` at the start or end of a real stoppage (play stopping or
  restarting a few seconds earlier or later than predicted).
- **The ball is missing on ~41 % of graded frames**, in long blocks. A football-specific ball detector on the video
  and a jersey-colour correction of the team of each track are the next steps with more time.
- **External matches are cleaner than the target**, even after degradation; the full-match score is expected below
  the external cross-validation.

## 6. Reproduction

```
git clone <this repository>
cd Tiki-Data-FC
pip install -r requirements.txt
# data: the organisers' bundle in ./data (or set MTAH_DATA_DIR); the organisers' repository next to this one
# (or set MTAH_CHALLENGE_DIR); external matches are downloaded by the scripts in train/
python run.py
```

`run.py` builds the external training data, trains the frame models, runs the two sequence models on CPU from
`models/seq/`, labels the match and writes `submission/Tiki-Data.csv`, then prints its SHA-256. Deterministic.
Retraining the sequence models themselves needs a GPU (`tools/seq_model.py`, deterministic).
